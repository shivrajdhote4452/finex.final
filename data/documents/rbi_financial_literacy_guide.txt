## Module 1: Household Cashflow and Budgeting
Page: 3
Prudent financial health rests on tracking personal cashflows and adhering to structured budgeting principles. The 50-30-20 principle provides an accessible framework: 50% of take-home income should fund essential living needs (housing, food, utilities), 30% for discretionary wants, and 20% strictly allocated towards debt reduction and long-term savings. Maintaining a written or digital budget prevents lifestyle inflation.

## Module 2: Prudent Borrowing and Debt Management
Page: 7
Borrowing should be restricted to productive assets such as housing or education that appreciate in value or enhance earning capacity. Unsecured consumer borrowing for depreciating goods at high interest rates (such as credit card revolving balances exceeding 36% to 42% per annum) risks debt traps. Total monthly EMI commitments should not exceed 40% to 50% of net household income.

## Module 3: Inflation and Purchasing Power
Page: 11
Inflation erodes the purchasing power of idle currency over time. At an annual inflation rate of 6%, the purchasing value of ₹1,00,000 is halved in approximately 12 years. Keeping entire savings in physical cash or low-interest zero-risk accounts guarantees negative real returns after adjusting for inflation and taxes. Investors must deploy savings into inflation-hedging financial assets.
