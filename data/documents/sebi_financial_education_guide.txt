## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Financial Planning
Page: 4
FinEx Educational Summary: Financial planning begins with establishing realistic goals and a disciplined savings rate (Official Source: SEBI Investor Education, https://investor.sebi.gov.in/). Before investing in market instruments, every individual should maintain an emergency fund and liquidity buffer equal to at least 3 to 6 months of basic living expenses in safe, liquid instruments such as bank fixed deposits or overnight mutual funds. Investing without an emergency reserve forces premature liquidation of equities during downturns.

## Chapter 2: Understanding Risk and Return in Securities
Page: 8
FinEx Educational Summary: Risk and expected return are inextricably linked in capital markets (Official Source: SEBI Investor Education, "How to Manage Investment Risks", https://investor.sebi.gov.in/investment_risk_managment.html). Equities represent ownership in companies and offer inflation-beating growth over long horizons (5+ years), but subject investors to short-term volatility. Debt securities and government bonds offer capital preservation and predictable coupon returns, but expose long-term holders to purchasing-power risk from inflation. A prudent asset allocation balances growth equities with stabilizing fixed income.

## Chapter 3: Asset Allocation and Diversification Principles
Page: 12
FinEx Educational Summary: Asset allocation and portfolio diversification are foundational risk-mitigation principles in securities markets (Official Source: SEBI Investor Education, "How to Manage Investment Risks", https://investor.sebi.gov.in/investment_risk_managment.html). Diversification involves spreading investments across different asset classes (equity, debt, and cash equivalents), market sectors, and issuers to reduce unsystematic (company-specific and sector-specific) risk. Diversification helps reduce some risks, but it does not eliminate market risk (systematic risk) and does not guarantee protection against investment losses or guarantee positive returns during market downturns. Under SEBI regulations, there are no statutory percentage holding limits or mandatory concentration caps for individual retail investors in their direct equity holdings; retail investors determine their own asset allocation based on their financial goals and risk tolerance. For mutual funds, current applicable regulations consolidated under the SEBI Master Circular for Mutual Funds dated March 20, 2026 (Circular No. HO/24/13/11(1)2026-IMD-POD-1/I/7602/2026, effective April 1, 2026, https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774024028162.pdf, historically originating in Regulation 44(1) and Clause 1 of the Seventh Schedule of the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996) enforce single-issuer concentration boundaries: a mutual fund scheme shall not invest more than 10% of its Net Asset Value (NAV) in equity shares or equity-related instruments of any single company. Scope and Exceptions: This statutory restriction applies specifically to actively managed mutual fund schemes to avoid concentration of scheme NAV in a single corporate issuer; it does NOT apply to individual retail investors. Furthermore, it is not a universal rule for all funds: Index Funds and Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) that passively replicate benchmark indices are exempt from the 10% single-company limit to replicate index constituents under SEBI portfolio concentration norms (subject to index-level capping rules); sectoral and thematic funds invest according to their designated mandates where sector concentration exceeds broad diversified limits; and debt instruments are governed by distinct credit-rating-based single-issuer limits under the debt exposure framework.

## Chapter 4: Systematic Investment Plans (SIP) and Compounding
Page: 16
FinEx Educational Summary: A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows retail investors to invest a predetermined amount periodically, enforcing rupee cost averaging (Official Source: SEBI Investor Education, https://investor.sebi.gov.in/). By buying more units when prices fall and fewer when prices rise, SIP eliminates the need to time the market. Compounding functions as an exponential force: starting 5 years earlier at age 25 with modest contributions yields substantially higher wealth than starting at age 35 with double the capital contribution.

## Chapter 5: Grievance Redressal and SCORES Portal
Page: 22
FinEx Educational Summary: All registered intermediaries (brokers, depository participants, mutual funds) are bound by SEBI regulations (Official Source: SEBI SCORES 2.0 Portal, https://scores.sebi.gov.in/ and SMART ODR Platform, https://smartodr.in/). In the event of unresolved disputes or service failures, investors should first lodge complaints directly with the entity. If unaddressed within 30 days, investors may escalate claims to the SEBI Complaints Redress System (SCORES 2.0) portal or initiate online dispute resolution (ODR) through the SMART ODR platform.
